# A teacher’s quick-start guide to Classroom Games

[Classroom Games](https://classroom-games.net/) has browser games, teacher tools and activity guides. These sample routines are suggestions you can adapt to your students, subject and class time.

## Choose your setup

**One teacher screen:** Open the [online games collection](https://classroom-games.net/play/) on a projector or smartboard. Students can answer aloud or play in teams; individual student devices are not needed for the board games.

**Student devices available:** Explore the [live classroom quiz](https://classroom-games.net/live/). Follow the on-screen room and joining instructions. Test the flow with your own device before class.

**Offline activity:** Browse the [game guides](https://classroom-games.net/games/). Check the materials and room setup for your chosen activity before starting.

## Routine 1: a short lesson review

1. Prepare five questions from the previous lesson, including one common misconception.
2. Open [Quiz Show](https://classroom-games.net/play/quiz-show/) and follow its setup instructions to add your questions.
3. Split the class into teams. Give everyone thinking time before a team answers.
4. Ask for the reason behind an answer, then explain any misconception.
5. End with one question each student answers independently.

Use the [review games collection](https://classroom-games.net/types/review-games/) to find alternatives for a longer session.

## Routine 2: a vocabulary warm-up

1. Choose a small set of words from today’s lesson.
2. Open [Guess the Word](https://classroom-games.net/play/guess-the-word/) or [Word Scramble](https://classroom-games.net/play/word-scramble/).
3. Give a clue or a sentence context before students guess.
4. Rotate turns, and ask students to use the solved word in a new sentence.

For more ideas, browse [vocabulary and spelling games](https://classroom-games.net/subjects/vocabulary-spelling/) or [ESL classroom games](https://classroom-games.net/subjects/esl/).

## Routine 3: a brain break between tasks

1. Pick an activity from the [brain breaks collection](https://classroom-games.net/types/brain-breaks/).
2. Read its instructions and choose a version that fits your room and students.
3. Show a short countdown with the [Classroom Timer](https://classroom-games.net/tools/timer/).
4. Explain how students will stop and return to their places when time ends.
5. Start the next lesson task with one clear instruction.

For days when you need a calmer activity, use the [quiet games collection](https://classroom-games.net/types/quiet-games/).

## Find a game that fits

Start with your purpose, then narrow the choice by grade and subject. The [educational games page](https://classroom-games.net/educational-games/) is a useful starting point for curriculum practice. The [no-prep collection](https://classroom-games.net/types/no-prep/) is useful when there is little time for setup.
